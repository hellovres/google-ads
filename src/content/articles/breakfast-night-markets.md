## The short answer

Chongqing eats early and late. Breakfast is a bowl of noodles or a steamed bun eaten quickly before work. Night food is skewers, hotpot and noodles shared after dark.

Plan one morning for a street breakfast and one evening for a market crawl. Both are cheap and both show a side of the city that restaurant meals miss.

## Breakfast

The classic breakfast is **xiaomian**: a bowl of noodles with chili, peanuts and pickles. It costs CNY 7–15 and takes ten minutes to eat.

Other common options:

- **Steamed buns:** pork, vegetable or red bean.
- **Youtiao:** fried dough sticks, often dipped in soy milk.
- **Soy milk:** sweet or savory, depending on the shop.
- **Rice rolls:** soft sheets with egg, pork or vegetables.
- **Congee:** plain rice porridge with pickles.

Look for a shop with a morning queue and a short menu. Many close by 10:30.

## Night food

After 20:00, the city shifts to skewers, hotpot and late noodles. The best areas are near residential compounds and metro exits rather than the main tourist streets.

What to order:

- Grilled skewers with cumin and chili.
- Fried potato with herbs.
- Stinky tofu.
- A late bowl of xiaomian.
- A sweet dessert such as bingfen.

## A one-day food route

**07:30:** Find a xiaomian shop near your hotel. Order a basic bowl with mild chili and a soy milk.

**12:00:** Eat a sit-down lunch in the neighborhood you are visiting. Keep it simple.

**18:30:** Have a light dinner or skip it if you plan a hotpot.

**21:00:** Walk a night food street, choose three or four items and finish with a dessert.

## Practical notes

- Carry tissues. Many small shops do not provide napkins.
- Bring hand sanitizer.
- Check the oil and the turnover at a stall.
- Keep a small cash reserve.
- Do not order more than you can finish. Portions are generous.

## The best food souvenir

A bag of Sichuan pepper and a jar of chili oil are easy to carry and useful at home. Buy from a busy shop or a supermarket rather than a tourist stall.

## The point of the route

Chongqing's food culture is built around time: a quick breakfast, a long lunch, a late dinner. Fit your day around those hours and the city becomes easier to read.
