## The short answer

Chongqing hotpot is a bubbling pot of beef tallow, chili and Sichuan pepper, surrounded by plates of meat, tofu, vegetables and offal. You cook each item yourself, dip it in sesame oil and eat it with a group.

Order a split pot if you are unsure about heat. Start with a small set of familiar ingredients. Add more once you understand the pace.

## The pot

The classic Chongqing broth is **red oil**, made with beef tallow, dried chilies and Sichuan pepper. It is rich, numbing and extremely hot. A **mandarin duck pot** divides the vessel into spicy and mild sections, which is the easiest choice for mixed groups.

Some restaurants offer tomato, mushroom or bone broth. These are useful for children or anyone who wants a break from the heat. They are not the traditional experience, but they make the meal work.

## The spice conversation

Say the number you want, not just "mild."

- **微辣 (wēi là):** mildly spicy. Still hot by most international standards.
- **中辣 (zhōng là):** medium. A serious step up.
- **特辣 (tè là):** very spicy.
- **不要辣 (bú yào là):** no chili. Useful for a separate dish, difficult for classic hotpot.

If you want a gentle introduction, ask for a split pot and eat mostly from the mild side. Do not assume the red side will be manageable because the menu says "mild."

## What to order

A balanced first order:

- **Beef slices:** quick to cook and easy to judge.
- **Lamb:** richer and slightly gamey.
- **Tofu skin:** soaks up the broth and cools quickly.
- **Potato:** starch that balances the heat.
- **Enoki mushrooms or oyster mushrooms:** texture and sweetness.
- **Leafy greens:** add them late so they do not overcook.
- **Hand-pulled noodles or rice:** a base for the final bowl.

Chongqing specialties include **tripe**, **duck intestine**, **blood tofu** and **brain**. Order them if you are curious, but do not feel obligated. A good hotpot is about the broth and the company, not a checklist of exotic ingredients.

## The dipping bowl

The standard dip is sesame oil, garlic and cilantro, sometimes with oyster sauce or vinegar. The oil coats each piece and cools it slightly. If the restaurant has a sauce station, keep it simple: sesame oil, garlic, a little chili and herbs.

Do not drink the broth. It is intensely salty and fatty by the end of the meal.

## How to cook

- Thin meat slices cook in seconds. Swish them through the boiling broth and remove them while still tender.
- Thick vegetables need several minutes.
- Leafy greens shrink quickly and can make the pot bitter if left too long.
- Use separate chopsticks for raw meat if the restaurant provides them.
- Keep the pot at a rolling boil, especially when adding raw meat.

## Etiquette

Hotpot is social. Share the pot, add ingredients for the table and do not fish around for a specific piece with your own chopsticks. Use the serving spoon if one is provided.

The meal is loud and informal. It is fine to ask for help, point at the menu and use a translation app.

## What it costs

A casual local hotpot costs around CNY 80–120 per person. A polished restaurant with better ingredients and English support can reach CNY 150–250. Drinks and premium beef add more.

Check whether the restaurant charges for the pot base, sauces or napkins. These are normal line items.

## The first-timer's order

If you want a safe but real introduction, order:

1. A split pot with mild and medium spice.
2. Beef slices, lamb, tofu skin, potato and a green vegetable.
3. Sesame oil, garlic and cilantro for the dip.
4. Rice or noodles at the end.
5. A cold drink and a bowl of plain rice to calm the heat.

That is enough food for two people. Add more only after the first round.
