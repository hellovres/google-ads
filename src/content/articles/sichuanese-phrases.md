## The short answer

Learn ten core phrases and keep the characters on your phone. Most people in Chongqing understand standard Mandarin, even when they reply in a local accent or Sichuanese.

Pinyin is included for pronunciation. Tone marks show the contour, but a translation app with audio is the best way to practise.

## When to use them

### At a restaurant

Start with **nǐ hǎo**, then point at the menu. Use **duō shao qián** for the price and **bú yào là** if you need no chili. At the end, say **mǎi dān**.

If the staff speak quickly, say **qǐng shuō màn yī diǎn** and show the phone.

### In a taxi

Show the destination in Chinese. Use **dì tiě zhàn zěn me zǒu** for the metro and **duō shao qián** for the fare. If the driver uses the meter, confirm that it is running.

### At a ticket counter

Ask **kě yǐ shuā kǎ ma** or **kě yǐ sǎo mǎ ma**. If the answer is unclear, show the payment app.

### In a shop

**duō shao qián** and **tài guì le** cover most bargaining situations. In malls and chain stores, prices are fixed.

## Pronunciation notes

- **q** sounds like a soft "ch."
- **x** sounds like "sh" with a smile.
- **c** sounds like "ts."
- **zh** sounds like "j" with the tongue curled back.
- The tone marks matter. A rising tone is different from a falling tone.

Do not worry about perfection. A clear attempt is usually appreciated, and people will often switch to simpler words or a translation app.

## Local flavor

**要得 (yào dé)** is a common Sichuanese way to say "okay" or "sure." **巴适 (bā shì)** means comfortable, good or satisfying. Use them with a smile, not as a performance.

## The five to memorize first

If you learn only five, choose:

1. **nǐ hǎo** — hello
2. **xiè xie** — thank you
3. **duō shao qián** — how much
4. **bú yào là** — no chili
5. **cè suǒ zài nǎ** — where is the toilet

Those five cover a surprising amount of a travel day.
