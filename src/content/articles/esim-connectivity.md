## The short answer

Buy a data-only travel eSIM before departure, install it at home and activate it on arrival. Keep your home SIM active for verification codes and calls. Download offline maps and a local map app as backup.

Connectivity is manageable, but the setup matters more than the brand.

## eSIM vs local SIM

### Travel eSIM

A travel eSIM is convenient. You install it before flying, activate it when you land and avoid a shop queue. Many plans are data-only, which is enough for maps, messaging and payments.

Check the China coverage, the network partner and whether the plan supports hotspot. Read the activation rules carefully.

### Local SIM

A local SIM can be cheaper for long stays and may include a Chinese phone number. It requires passport registration at a carrier shop. The process takes time and the staff may not speak English.

A Chinese number is useful for some local services, but it is not essential for a short tourist trip.

## Install before you fly

1. Buy the eSIM and save the QR code or activation details.
2. Install the profile while you have stable Wi-Fi.
3. Label it clearly in your phone settings.
4. Turn off data roaming on your home SIM.
5. Activate the eSIM on arrival.
6. Test a map, a message and a payment app.

Keep the eSIM QR code accessible in case you need to reinstall it.

## What may not work

Some foreign apps and services can be slow or unavailable on mainland networks. Plan around it:

- Download offline maps before departure.
- Install a local map app such as Amap.
- Save hotel addresses in Chinese.
- Use messaging apps that work for your contacts.
- Keep a paper copy of key bookings.

Do not rely on a single app for navigation.

## Data use

Maps, translation and payment apps use modest data. Video and cloud backups use much more. A 5–10 GB plan is enough for a one-week trip with normal use. Heavy video calls need more.

Turn off automatic photo backup and app updates on mobile data.

## At the airport

Test the connection before leaving the terminal. Open a map, load a translation and send a message. If the eSIM does not activate, use the airport Wi-Fi to troubleshoot.

Keep your home SIM active for bank verification codes. Turn off data roaming on it to avoid charges.

## Backup plan

- A second eSIM or a physical SIM.
- Airport Wi-Fi.
- Hotel Wi-Fi.
- Offline maps and saved screenshots.
- A printed hotel address.

## The setup that works

Use a travel eSIM for data, keep your home number for verification, download offline maps and save the hotel address in Chinese. That combination covers most arrival-day problems.
